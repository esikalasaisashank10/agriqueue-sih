The wait starts at 4 in the morning and nobody can tell you when it ends.

Farmers load the trolley on rumour. The centre's opening date, the day's capacity and the position in the queue are all things you can only learn by standing in it.

What AgriQueue does

One registration, one slot, one status screen.

The five things the problem statement asks for, built as one flow rather than five features a farmer has to discover.

Step 01

Register once, with what you already carry

Mobile number and OTP. Land record and crop details are pulled from the state registry, so a farmer is not asked to type anything they can't read back.

Mobile OTPPattadar passbookBank account on file
Step 02

Book a slot at a centre that has room

Each centre publishes its daily weighment capacity. Slots are offered in one-hour bands and close when the day is full, so a farmer never travels to a centre that cannot take the load.

Capacity-awareReschedule freeNearest first
Step 03

Watch the queue from the village

The token shows how many trolleys are ahead and when the turn is expected. The estimate is recomputed from how fast the weighbridge is actually moving today, not from the booking sheet.

Live positionReal throughput ETAWorks offline
Step 04

Every status change reaches the farmer, not just the file

Arrival, weighment, quality decision and payment each trigger a message in the farmer's language. A rejection always carries the reason and the appeal window, so the decision can be argued instead of absorbed.

SMSApp pushWhatsAppVoice call
Step 05

The centre stops being a crowd

Officers see the booked load for the day before it arrives, can shift the whole afternoon's slots with one broadcast when the weighbridge stalls, and can spot no-shows early enough to pull the next farmer forward.

Capacity planningDelay broadcastAudit trail
Works without a smartphone

The farmers who wait longest are usually the ones without a data plan.

Every function in the app also works as a text message and as a phone call in the local language. Nothing important lives only inside the app.

What changes

The same procurement, with the waiting taken out of it.

For the farmer

  • Travels once, at a time the centre can actually take the load.
  • Keeps the MSP instead of selling cheap to avoid another day of waiting.
  • Gets a rejection reason in writing, with a way to contest it.

For the centre

  • Knows the day's committed load before the gate opens.
  • Fewer trolleys parked overnight, less crowding and fewer disputes.
  • Weighment, grading and payment recorded once, at the point it happens.

For the department

  • State-wide view of arrivals, wait times and rejection reasons by centre.
  • Capacity moved to the centres that are actually congested.
  • A digital audit trail from token to bank credit.

Farmer side

Progressive web app and Android build, offline-first so the token and last known status survive with no signal.

Queue engine

Node.js services with Redis for the live queue, PostgreSQL as the record of truth, WebSockets for position updates.

Messaging

SMS gateway, WhatsApp Business API, push notifications and an IVR tree sharing one template store across languages.

Integrations

State procurement portals and e-NAM for centre and rate data, PFMS-style payment status, DigiLocker for land records.

Centre hardware

Runs on the tablet or desktop already at the weighbridge; a printed token keeps the queue working if the network drops.
